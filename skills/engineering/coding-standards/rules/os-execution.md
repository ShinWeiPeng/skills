# OS and execution environment

| Rule ID | Strength | Applicable conditions | Program requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| OS-CAP-001 | MUST | Code uses OS facilities. | Required capabilities, allowed call environments and waiting limits shall match the confirmed platform. Fixed conditions shall be checked at design/build time; necessary deployment/runtime conditions shall be checked at initialization or before use. Unsatisfied conditions shall reject the affected operation and report through the abnormal-event contract. Alternatives shall be previously confirmed and preserve required semantics and safety; other capabilities shall not be assumed equivalent. |
| OS-EXEC-001 | MUST | Code has a confirmed execution design. | Work shall run in its assigned execution unit with the declared responsibility, module mapping and call environment. Changes shall not silently move execution across boundaries. Logical modules and processing stages shall not imply one Task per module; selected stage handoffs shall preserve bounded capacity, scheduling, timing and lifecycle contracts. |
| OS-CHAN-001 | MUST | Data, events or messages cross Task boundaries. | Handoffs shall use a communication facility supported by the target OS, with declared sender/receiver, purpose, call environment, finite capacity, ordering and delivery contract. Undeclared shared globals or direct mutation of another Task's private state shall not replace handoff. Enqueued, received and processed shall be distinct. Record retention or coalescing shall follow the confirmed purpose and retain necessary progress/state. Full, wait, timeout and Stop behavior shall follow the contract. Value/reference transfer shall define holding/release responsibility; a notification shall not claim per-record delivery, and channel transfer shall not replace lifetime protection. Existing abnormal-event overwrite and persistent module-fault rules remain applicable. |
| OS-SYNC-001 | MUST | Concurrent activities share data or wait for other activities. | Private mutable state shall be modified by its owner; other Tasks shall request changes through OS channels. Shared access permissions, synchronization and reclamation shall be explicit. Locks shall cover declared objects and scopes with bounded holding/waiting and supported OS behavior. Multiple locks/channel waits shall use a checkable deadlock-avoidance strategy; timing shall account for priority inversion. Failure, timeout and cancellation shall release this operation's held locks/resources while preserving data still in use. ISR and other restricted paths shall obey permitted calls and blocking limits; acquisition failure shall use the channel/abnormal contract. Shared data need not universally use mutexes. |
| OS-RES-001 | MUST | Code allocates or uses OS resources. | Task stacks, queues/mailboxes, synchronization objects, timers, handles, shared data and in-flight references shall obey declared finite capacities, owners, allocation time, budget basis and reclamation conditions. Total simultaneous demand shall include OS overhead and count shared blocks once. Estimates shall not claim measured margins; required measurements shall support the deployed budget. Creation failure/exhaustion shall not report success and shall use confirmed rejection, backpressure, discard or Stop behavior. Unbounded growth or silent strategy changes are forbidden. Cancellation/restart shall reclaim resources without repeated accumulation; numeric limits are project-specific. |
| OS-TIME-001 | MUST | Paths have timing, throughput or freshness requirements. | Hard, soft and ordinary workloads shall follow confirmed triggering, frequency/burst, priority, execution/wait budgets and required core assignments. End-to-end costs shall include queueing, aggregation, lock blocking, handoff, interrupts and OS overhead. Average time or CPU utilization alone shall not prove a deadline. Real-time analysis shall use a supported scheduler model and evidence-backed conservative budgets. Implementation shall supply required bounds/evidence. Hard misses fail; soft tolerance shall have a quantified contract. Normal, peak and relevant slow-consumer conditions shall meet the required criteria; limits come from project/platform facts. |
| OS-LIFE-001 | MUST | Tasks or OS resources initialize, start, stop, cancel or restart. | A coordinator, lifecycle states and ordering shall govern initialization, readiness, start, Stop, cancellation and restart. Created shall not imply ready; required channels/receivers shall be ready before admission. Partial creation failure shall safely release this operation's resources and report. Stop shall first close admission, then drain or cancel within the declared bound through OS coordination and completion replies. Timeout shall expose unfinished work; data or OS objects still used by live activities shall not be reclaimed. Restart shall isolate or finish old activity and handle stale messages/references before admitting new work. Failures/restarts shall not cause unbounded retries or resource accumulation; existing Stop and module-state contracts remain applicable. |
